How we know this

Every number on this page comes from a public government source, except the coast crossing, which we estimate and label as an estimate.

What you seeWhere it comes fromHow much to trust it
Storm position, winds, pressure, warnings, surge heights, forecast points, coneNational Hurricane Center (NOAA) advisories and forecast mapsOfficial. Full advisories every 6 hours, updates every 3 hours or sooner.
Alerts for your address (warnings, local statements, flood watches)National Weather Service alerts for your exact pointOfficial, issued by your local NWS office.
When tropical-storm winds may arriveNHC arrival-time maps and forecast wind fieldOfficial inputs. The wind field marks the farthest reach in each direction, so it leans cautious.
Storm surge flooding layerNHC Potential Storm Surge Flooding mapOfficial reasonable worst case. Issued when surge watches or warnings are in effect.
Your address's locationU.S. Census Bureau geocoderGood to the block. Not a rooftop survey.
Estimated coast crossingOur calculation: where NHC's straight-line track between forecast points first meets the shoreline (Census coastline)An estimate, not an NHC product. The real crossing can land anywhere in the cone; the cone is NHC's measure of that uncertainty.
Towns and landmarks in the tableU.S. Census Gazetteer; a short hand-checked list of beach landmarksFor orientation only.
Base mapU.S. Geological Survey, The National MapFor orientation only.

Not used: news write-ups, social media, and individual computer-model runs ("spaghetti plots"). Your address is sent only to the Census Bureau to find its coordinates, and those coordinates only to the National Weather Service for alerts. We store nothing.

Not an official forecast. Always follow the National Hurricane Center (nhc.noaa.gov), your local National Weather Service office, and your local emergency managers. Hurricane preparation: ready.gov/hurricanes.
